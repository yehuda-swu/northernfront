NORTHERN FRONT
EDUCATOR GUIDE

A structured history and decision making activity about Israel's northern border and successive Israeli governments' responses to armed threats emerging from Lebanon.

16 TO 17Recommended pupil age
12 TO 15Minutes for gameplay
5Historical chapters
Learning outcomesDeliveryChapter notesDiscussionSourcesImage rights

LEARNING OUTCOMES

By the end of the activity, pupils should be able to:

  1. Describe the broad shift from PLO operations in southern Lebanon to Hezbollah's emergence and influence.
  2. Place the 1978 operation, the 1982 war, the rise of Hezbollah, the 2000 withdrawal, the 2006 war and the 2024 operation in chronological order.
  3. Explain why Israeli decision makers weighed civilian safety, military security and diplomacy.
  4. Distinguish between historical actions, simulated choices and untested alternative outcomes.
  5. Identify the purpose of UNIFIL, the Blue Line and UN Security Council Resolution 1701.

SUGGESTED DELIVERY

Important: This is not a test of a single correct decision. Each choice shows possible tradeoffs. The game does not predict what would have happened if a different decision had been taken.

For a classroom, display the game on a shared screen, use the Classroom control to enlarge text, and invite students to choose between A, B and C. The educator can pause before revealing the historical outcome. For individual work, students can make their own choices and download a short decision record at the end.

Sound is optional. Browsers generally require a user action before playing audio. The activity remains fully usable without sound. The two short historical video clips begin automatically on their chapter briefing pages, without sound because browsers normally restrict automatic audio. Pupils may use the player controls to enable sound or pause playback. For a 15 minute class, watch only one clip in full or use them as follow up material.

CHAPTER 01 · MARCH 1978

THE PLO THREAT

Historical context: Following the relocation of Palestinian armed groups from Jordan to Lebanon, attacks and reprisals intensified. The PLO claimed responsibility for the deadly Coastal Road attack on 11 March 1978. Israel entered southern Lebanon on 14 to 15 March.

Historical outcome: Operation Litani was followed by UN Resolutions 425 and 426 and the creation of UNIFIL. Israel withdrew in June 1978, but tensions continued.

Teaching emphasis: Why could a short military operation fail to remove a continuing cross border threat? Lebanon's government denied involvement in the Coastal Road attack.

Historical image dossiers: Avivim (1970): 12 people killed including nine children. Kiryat Shmona (1974): 18 killed including eight children. Maalot (1974): 22 pupils killed at the school, with other victims earlier that day. Counts of injured people vary across references. Keep the three attacks distinct from the immediate March 1978 trigger, the Coastal Road attack. The Maalot photograph of a covered casualty is hidden until a viewer explicitly requests it.

CHAPTER 02 · JUNE 1982

THE FIRST LEBANON WAR

Historical context: Israeli Ambassador Shlomo Argov was shot in London on 3 June 1982 by the Abu Nidal organisation, which was a rival of the PLO. Renewed exchanges of fire followed. Israel began its invasion on 6 June.

Historical outcome: Israeli forces advanced to Beirut. The PLO leadership and fighters ultimately left the city. The war caused major loss of life and destruction. Phalangist militiamen carried out the Sabra and Shatila massacres. The Israeli Kahan Commission concluded that Israel bore indirect responsibility for failing to prevent the atrocity.

Teaching emphasis: Distinguish the stated initial military aims from the eventual scale of the operation. Examine the consequences of joining an existing civil war.

CHAPTER 03 · JULY 1993

THE RISE OF HEZBOLLAH

Historical context: Hezbollah emerged in the early 1980s during the Lebanon conflict, with significant Iranian backing. Amal was a separate Lebanese Shi’a movement. Hassan Nasrallah became Hezbollah’s leader in 1992. In July 1993, Hezbollah rocket attacks and clashes with Israeli forces intensified.

Historical outcome: Israel launched Operation Accountability from 25 to 31 July 1993, carrying out air and artillery strikes. Civilians were killed and displaced, especially in Lebanon. A subsequent understanding intended to avoid attacks on civilians did not end the conflict.

Teaching emphasis: Explain the distinction between the PLO and Hezbollah. Discuss the effect of a prolonged military presence, Iran’s role and the limits of deterrence. The short automatically playing news video is a modern retrospective rather than 1993 footage. It begins muted and can be paused.

CHAPTER 04 · MAY 2000

ISRAEL WITHDRAWS

Historical context: After a partial withdrawal in 1985, Israeli forces and the South Lebanon Army maintained a security zone. Hezbollah attacks and the cost of prolonged deployment were major concerns.

Historical outcome: Israel completed its withdrawal in May 2000. The UN verified withdrawal using the Blue Line. Hezbollah remained armed. The Blue Line is not an internationally agreed border.

Teaching emphasis: Why might withdrawal be strategically valuable even when new threats remain? Discuss the effects on the South Lebanon Army and families who fled to Israel.

CHAPTER 05 · JULY 2006

THE SECOND LEBANON WAR

Historical context: On 12 July, Hezbollah attacked Israeli forces and captured two soldiers while firing rockets. Israel launched major air and ground operations.

Historical outcome: The war lasted 34 days and caused deaths, displacement and severe damage in both countries. Resolution 1701 called for a cessation of hostilities, Israeli withdrawal, deployment of Lebanese forces and expanded UNIFIL responsibilities.

Teaching emphasis: What are the strengths and limits of air power? What is needed for ceasefire arrangements to remain effective?

Seven aspects of UN Resolution 1701: cessation of hostilities; Israeli withdrawal; deployment of the Lebanese Armed Forces; disarmament of armed groups; restriction on unauthorised arms transfers; an area between the Blue Line and Litani River free of unauthorised armed personnel and weapons; and an expanded UNIFIL role. Adoption of the resolution did not guarantee full implementation of every provision.

Visual archive: a map of Israeli locations affected by Katyusha rocket attacks, photographs of Ehud Goldwasser and Eldad Regev, an IDF photograph, and the supplied UN Security Council photograph.

CHAPTER 06 · SEPTEMBER 2024

OPERATION NORTHERN ARROWS

Historical context: Hezbollah began attacks on northern Israel on 8 October 2023. Israeli and Lebanese civilians were displaced in significant numbers amid repeated exchanges of fire.

Historical outcome: Israel intensified operations in September 2024, killed Hezbollah leader Hassan Nasrallah on 27 September, and began ground operations at the end of September and beginning of October. A cessation of hostilities took effect on 27 November 2024. The agreement did not by itself guarantee lasting security.

Teaching emphasis: Discuss Israel's objective of enabling its northern residents to return and the humanitarian impact on both sides of the border.

DISCUSSION QUESTIONS

  1. What changed between Israel's confrontation with the PLO and its confrontation with Hezbollah?
  2. How did Hezbollah’s emergence and growth alter Israel’s security challenge after the 1982 invasion?
  3. Why might governments prefer military action when diplomacy appears ineffective?
  4. What are the dangers of operations that expand beyond their original objectives?
  5. What did the UN expect UNIFIL and Resolution 1701 to achieve?
  6. How can leaders know when a threat has been sufficiently reduced for civilians to return?
Facilitation: Ask pupils to provide evidence for their answers. Encourage them to separate what is known to have happened from what they believe might have happened.

ACCURACY & IMAGE RIGHTS

Educational reconstructions: The adviser remarks, decision menus and possible tradeoffs are written for the simulation. They are not verbatim historical quotations or models of alternative history.

Maps: The four map files supplied for this activity are symbolic visual references. Armed group emblems do not represent exact territorial control. Map markers and arrows show broad directions, not georeferenced operational positions.

Archive photos: The original archive images in the package were extracted from the user supplied Northern Arrows presentation. Additional photographs from the latest uploads are used in Chapter 2 cabinet and reveal screens and in Chapter 5 historical reveal. Some slides may contain third party imagery, including news photographs. Confirm the right to publish them on a public GitHub Pages site. If permission cannot be confirmed, replace the files named archive_1978.webp, archive_1982.webp, archive_2000.webp, archive_2006.webp and archive_2024.webp with images you are authorised to use.

Sources: The 2024 chapter describes events through the November 2024 cessation of hostilities. It does not claim to assess later developments. Recheck the text before adapting the activity to ongoing events.

New user supplied archive media: The game includes six new user supplied historical photographs and the Katyusha map from the original presentation, alongside two excerpts compressed from uploaded video files. The 1982 excerpt has an AP watermark and the Hezbollah news explainer features broadcaster branding. Ownership and public publishing rights have not been verified. Please confirm permission before making the game publicly accessible on GitHub Pages.

Sensitive content: One photograph depicts the transport of a covered casualty. The game hides it behind an explicit content notice. Educators should consider their group before opening the image. The game does not show graphic images by default.

Screen time: The clips autoplay silently in their chapter introductions, but are not required for the six chapter experience. Watching both videos in full extends the lesson beyond the usual target duration.
